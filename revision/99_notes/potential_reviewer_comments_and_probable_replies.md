## Coverage audit of current artifact
- **Well covered:** major points on circularity, proxy labels, baseline tuning, biological validity, pseudotime mismatch, scalability scope, statistics, interpretation controls, mixed performance claims, architecture consistency, and reproducibility package availability.
- **Partially covered:** manuscript terminology precision (e.g., disentanglement vs specialised latents) and graph-construction dependence.
- **Bottom line:** the current reviewer-comment set is strong but not exhaustive.
- **Missing / should be added:** explicit source-traceable additions on single-head vs multi-head wording, seed-policy boundaries, IB dimension rationale, NB-decoder/preprocessing compatibility, known-`k` K-means evaluation assumptions, venue/package consistency, and concise dataset-level auditability table.
- **Too speculative / deprioritize:** atlas-level speed/quality claims without additional experiments or full venue-compliance mapping for each potential journal variant.

## Additional plausible concerns not fully covered
1. **Novelty and positioning**: distinguish the graph-attention, VAE, hyperbolic-geometry, and information-bottleneck contributions from adjacent single-cell methods.
2. **Dataset preprocessing dependence and graph bias**: clarify whether performance advantages could track fixed preprocessing choices.
3. **Metric suitability for biological utility**: explain how clustering, DRE, and LSE metrics connect to biological use rather than proxy optimization alone.
4. **Terminology coherence across sections**: “disentangled/specialised/hierarchical” should be used with consistent definitions.
5. **Manuscript density and readability**: acronym/metric volume, claim hierarchy, and figure/table placement may need streamlining.
6. **Editorial/package workflow**: tracked-changes, filenames, package checklist, source availability, and target-venue consistency.
7. **NB decoder + preprocessing contract**: explicit size-factor/offset and normalization compatibility.
8. **Hyperbolic loss and dimensionality rationale**: why the 2D IB bottleneck and Lorentz loss are retained and how they complement `d=10` downstream.
9. **Known-cluster-number advantage**: K-means uses the Leiden cluster count, which may simplify the clustering comparison.
10. **Head-count and tuning assumptions**: single-head/multi-head language and sensitivity language.
